# Maz Works Client Acquisition

A lightweight operating playbook for finding and converting early Maz Works clients without turning the business into a generic AI agency.

## Positioning

Lead with the business problem and outcome. Technology comes second.

Good starting problems:

- enquiries are being lost or followed up inconsistently
- staff repeat the same admin every day
- a website does not make the next action obvious
- information is copied between tools manually
- customer support or complaint workflows are messy
- business-development opportunities are difficult to research or track
- AI could help, but important actions still need human control

## Weekly loop

### 1. Target

Choose a small number of businesses where a specific problem is visible or reasonably discoverable. Prefer relevance over volume.

### 2. Research

Spend a few minutes understanding:

- what the business sells
- how customers currently contact them
- where the obvious friction is
- which systems or channels they already use
- which Maz Works project is the closest proof

### 3. Outreach

Keep the first message short and specific.

Structure:

1. mention the real thing you noticed
2. explain the likely friction or missed opportunity
3. suggest one useful improvement
4. offer to build a free live demo for a suitable problem
5. offer a Microsoft Teams walkthrough if seeing it live would help
6. link only the most relevant proof

Do not lead with AI, agents, LLMs or a long capability list unless the prospect is already asking for that.

### 4. Free live demo

The free step should make the idea tangible, not become unpaid production work.

For a suitable problem:

- understand what happens now
- choose the smallest useful slice of the workflow
- build a near-working demonstration around the real use case
- use realistic inputs where safe, without exposing private client data
- show what remains mocked, manual or out of scope
- explain what the paid implementation would include and exclude

The demo should be close enough for the prospect to understand the experience and value, while staying clearly separate from the finished production system.

### 5. Demo delivery

Offer two simple routes:

- send the live demo link for the prospect to explore themselves
- run a Microsoft Teams screen-share walkthrough and explain the full flow

A Teams walkthrough is useful when the prospect wants to ask questions, see edge cases or understand how the system fits their existing workflow.

Do not force a discovery call before showing useful work where the problem is already clear.

### 6. Scope and price

Use the public offers as entry anchors:

- Quick Win — £150 fixed
- Website Launch — from £299
- Growth System — from £499
- Optional support — from £49/month

Agree the actual scope before paid work starts. Larger, unusual or third-party-heavy builds are quoted separately.

Do not quietly expand a starter package into unlimited work.

### 7. Delivery

During the build:

- communicate changes clearly
- reuse proven tools and open-source components where sensible
- keep secrets and client data out of public repos
- test the real path the client will use
- add approval, validation, suppression or fallback controls where automation can create meaningful risk
- leave a simple handover

### 8. After successful delivery

Ask for, where appropriate:

- direct feedback
- permission to use the work as a case study
- a short approved testimonial
- a referral to one relevant business
- the next genuine improvement worth considering

Do not manufacture a retainer if there is no ongoing need.

## Lead tracker

Keep a small tracker with:

- prospect
- business
- problem spotted
- contact route
- most relevant proof
- first contact date
- follow-up date
- response
- live demo status
- Teams walkthrough requested/completed
- proposal
- won/lost
- next action

A spreadsheet or simple existing tool is enough until the process itself becomes the bottleneck.

## Follow-up rule

Use one or two polite follow-ups after the first personalised message. Stop if there is no interest.

## Proof hierarchy

Use proof in this order:

1. relevant contract/client work
2. live product solving a similar problem
3. a tailored live demo of the prospect's use case
4. public code or technical evidence when the buyer cares
5. professional background where it supports trust

For Maz Works today:

- Scrap Finance Partners demonstrates contract client delivery across website, lead workflow and guarded automation
- JobFilter demonstrates construction-focused opportunity and workflow automation
- Agent Nudge demonstrates software/product thinking around controlled AI workflows
- MAZ Pocket demonstrates hardware/software integration and experimentation
- complaints/escalations experience demonstrates investigation, communication, ownership and process improvement

## Guardrails

Never claim:

- guaranteed leads
- guaranteed contract awards
- guaranteed revenue
- invented ROI
- fabricated testimonials
- invented client counts
- a large team that does not exist

For AI-assisted work, prefer controlled partial automation when judgment or customer impact matters. Useful controls include:

- draft before send
- explicit approval
- dry-run mode
- validation
- suppression lists
- bounded batch sizes
- audit/history
- manual fallback

## Simple outreach template

> Hi — I noticed [specific thing]. It looks like [specific process/problem] may be creating extra admin or missed follow-up. I build small websites and automations around problems like this. If useful, I can put together a small live demo around that exact workflow before you commit to anything, and I can walk you through it on Teams if that is easier. Here is the closest example of my work: [one relevant link].

Rewrite this for every prospect. Do not mass-send it unchanged.
