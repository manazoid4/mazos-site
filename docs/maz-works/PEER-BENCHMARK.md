# Maz Works Peer Benchmark

Research date: 2026-08-13

## Purpose

This benchmark looks at small, independent software studios and founder-led businesses adjacent to Maz Works: custom software, workflow automation, AI implementation, in-house products, and commissioned client work.

It deliberately excludes large consultancies and interchangeable lead-generation “AI agencies.” The comparison is based on current first-party websites. It evaluates the public story and information architecture, not company revenue, traffic, or delivery quality that cannot be independently verified.

## Bottom line

Maz Works should not copy one competitor. The strongest direction is a composite:

- **Side Quest Studios:** problem-led positioning and a clean separation between owned products and selective client work.
- **Sidejar:** an immediately understandable hybrid studio model and compact project labeling.
- **Taylor Make It:** unmistakable founder accountability, plain service examples, and real screenshots.
- **Adee Labs:** shipped products and public usage proof before any client-services pitch.
- **DejaDo:** a bounded entry offer with explicit fit, scope, and exclusion language.
- **KernelTheory:** client ownership and a case-study narrative built around before, risk, intervention, and after.
- **Bluespace Studio:** business-workflow language and practical scope discipline.

Maz Works already has a stronger raw story than many of these peers: Manazir has both owned products and a verified commissioned client. The site should make that evidence visible before explaining every service, process, or price.

The differentiating public position should remain:

> Manazir Hussain builds useful software, AI tools and automation around real problems.

The commercial expression is secondary:

> Show me the problem. I’ll build a small tailored demonstration first.

## Detailed evaluation

### 1. Sidejar — closest hybrid-studio model

Official source: [Sidejar](https://sidejar.studio/)

- **First five seconds:** “small product studio,” AI consulting, custom software, and its own products are all explained immediately. The visitor does not have to infer whether it is an agency or a product company.
- **Proof hierarchy:** the page lists in-house and client projects with explicit labels, then adds named testimonials. This is credible because the relationship type is visible.
- **Service framing:** three simple lanes—AI consulting, custom software, and owned products—are easier to understand than a long capability grid.
- **Founder visibility:** the full six-person team is named and pictured, including the co-founders and delivery roles.
- **Commercial path:** a low-friction email route closes the page. It does not force a complex funnel.
- **Editorial pattern:** a compact, type-led single page uses short sections and project links instead of inflated case-study copy.
- **Maz Works verdict:** emulate the clear owned/client labeling and simplicity. Improve on it by giving JobFilter and Scrap Finance Partners much more visual and narrative weight than smaller experiments.

### 2. Side Quest Studios — closest positioning model

Official source: [Side Quest Studios](https://www.sidequeststudios.io/)

- **First five seconds:** it promises software for operators and immediately connects that promise to costly workflows. That is more concrete than leading with “AI solutions.”
- **Proof hierarchy:** three live products appear before client services, each tied to a narrow user and painful workflow.
- **Service framing:** workflow automation, custom product builds, and AI integration are expressed as situations the buyer recognizes.
- **Founder visibility:** the site presents a tight team but does not foreground named people. This is a gap Maz Works should beat.
- **Commercial path:** the contact form asks what kind of problem exists and asks the visitor to describe the friction. This is well aligned with consultative scoping.
- **Editorial pattern:** bold section labels, declarative headlines, live/building statuses, short principles, and a single long-page narrative.
- **Maz Works verdict:** emulate “specific operator problem → live product → selective client work.” Do not copy the anti-agency slogans, fantasy-themed CTA language, or unsupported financial framing.

### 3. Bluespace Studio — best business-workflow language

Official sources: [About](https://www.bluespace.studio/about/) and [Work](https://www.bluespace.studio/work/)

- **First five seconds:** the studio builds software around real operations and names the familiar failure state: spreadsheets, inboxes, and tools that do not fit.
- **Proof hierarchy:** its work index uses a concise context/build/outcome pattern and distinguishes specific client work from grouped examples.
- **Service framing:** portals, CRMs, dashboards, internal tools, and automation are framed as replacements for manual glue—not as technology badges.
- **Founder visibility:** Priyanshu is named, and the page states that the founder remains involved from the first conversation through follow-on work.
- **Commercial path:** “describe the workflow” is a better prompt than “book a discovery call” alone. Call and email remain available.
- **Editorial pattern:** conventional multi-page structure, restrained headings, short paragraphs, and a clear work index.
- **Maz Works verdict:** emulate problem-first service language and scope discipline. Avoid reproducing its large SEO-oriented footer taxonomy before Maz Works has enough case studies to justify it.

### 4. Taylor Make It — best solo-founder clarity

Official source: [Taylor Make It](https://www.taylormakeit.com/)

- **First five seconds:** the offer—websites, apps, and tools for growing businesses—is plain, followed immediately by “I design and build.”
- **Proof hierarchy:** three selected projects use real screenshots, brief responsibility statements, and live links.
- **Service framing:** service examples such as quote generators, portals, dashboards, booking systems, and workflow assistants make abstract capabilities tangible.
- **Founder visibility:** a portrait, name, location, current role, and first-person copy make accountability unmistakable.
- **Commercial path:** one repeated “Start a Project” action and a visible email address keep the path simple.
- **Editorial pattern:** clean single-page sequencing—offer, services, selected work, process, founder, contact—with little conceptual overhead.
- **Maz Works verdict:** emulate the founder clarity, example-based service copy, real screenshots, and direct contact. Keep Maz Works pricing lower in the hierarchy because the £150 founding offer is deliberately bounded and should not define the whole identity.

### 5. Leanline Studios — strongest full commercial package

Official sources: [Leanline Studios](https://leanline-studios.com/) and [Portfolio](https://leanline-studios.com/portfolio)

- **First five seconds:** the page states AI automation, software development, product incubation, a founder-led network model, and a shipping promise.
- **Proof hierarchy:** testimonials appear early; a portfolio follows with status/duration, problem summaries, and technology.
- **Service framing:** “launch,” “fix,” and “automate” are useful outcome buckets. They translate more naturally than a list of frameworks.
- **Founder visibility:** direct founder involvement and the specialist-network model are stated repeatedly, though the individual could be more visually prominent.
- **Commercial path:** free office hours, a booking link, package prices, custom engagements, and email give buyers several entry points.
- **Editorial pattern:** comprehensive landing-page sections, cards, project rows, price blocks, and repeated conversion points.
- **Maz Works verdict:** adapt the launch/fix/automate framing and clear engagement boundaries. Avoid its breadth and repetition; Maz Works should feel like one capable builder with selected proof, not a service catalogue.

### 6. Adee Labs — strongest work-before-services sequence

Official source: [Adee Labs](https://www.adeelabs.com/)

- **First five seconds:** it identifies itself as an independent product studio and supports the promise with shipped-product usage immediately.
- **Proof hierarchy:** selected products, public usage/rating signals, and teams worked with all appear before the partner-services offer.
- **Service framing:** product design, web/mobile apps, workflow plugins/extensions, and useful AI are expressed through what people can do with the result.
- **Founder visibility:** the studio identity is clear, but the individual behind it is not prominent. Maz Works should beat this by naming Manazir immediately.
- **Commercial path:** a limited partner-work section and direct email follow the portfolio, so the service sale feels earned by the work.
- **Editorial pattern:** numbered chapters, one flagship product, a compact product index, large proof figures, a short origin story, and one final conversation prompt.
- **Maz Works verdict:** emulate the sequence `owned work → public proof → selective partner work`. Never reproduce usage figures, client logos, scarcity, or ratings without Maz Works-specific evidence and permission.

### 7. Dorio — strongest pain-first client journey

Official source: [Dorio](https://dorio.io/)

- **First five seconds:** manual work, broken tools, and disconnected systems establish the problem; fixed scope and direct engineer access explain the delivery model.
- **Proof hierarchy:** founder, client marks, numerical results, case studies, testimonials, then prices. The hierarchy is commercially complete but overlong.
- **Service framing:** three buyer-recognisable situations—repeated manual work, failing software, and software needing smarter features—are more useful than a generic services grid.
- **Founder visibility:** Mahmudul Hassan is named, pictured, and explicit that he designs and leads each system while trusted specialists add capacity.
- **Commercial path:** a free call or written problem submission leads to a diagnostic or fixed-price implementation; ownership and handover are explained.
- **Editorial pattern:** long-form conversion modules, large quantified claims, repeated calls to action, situation cards, case studies, fit criteria, pricing, and FAQ.
- **Maz Works verdict:** emulate direct builder access, problem-shaped offers, honest fit checks, and ownership. Avoid Dorio’s urgency, repeated CTAs, page length, and any numerical claim that Maz Works cannot substantiate.

### 8. DejaDo — best bounded-offer explanation

Official source: [DejaDo](https://dejadoit.com/)

- **First five seconds:** it names recurring SME admin—invoice chasing, enquiry replies, and onboarding—and promises plain English, fixed scope, and an honest no-build answer.
- **Proof hierarchy:** an explicit before/after workflow illustration demonstrates the concept, and the page carefully labels it as illustrative rather than client evidence.
- **Service framing:** `Lite` covers one bounded repeatable problem; `Pro` covers messier cross-system work that requires diagnosis. The distinction protects scope without sounding hostile.
- **Founder visibility:** Jack Power is named, pictured, and explains why the business exists in first person.
- **Commercial path:** a 15-minute fit call or written Process Snapshot can result in Lite, Pro, or a recommendation not to build.
- **Editorial pattern:** one repeated “admin loop” concept, a restrained before/after diagram, two engagement tracks, four delivery steps, and a founder close.
- **Maz Works verdict:** this is the best reference for presenting the bounded demo, £150 implementation, exclusions, and separately quoted additions. Maz Works must lead with stronger shipped proof than DejaDo presently shows.

### 9. KernelTheory — strongest ownership and control story

Official sources: [KernelTheory](https://kerneltheory.com/) and [Quik Referral case study](https://kerneltheory.com/quik-referral.html)

- **First five seconds:** the studio helps founders turn vague or broken products into working systems they understand and own.
- **Proof hierarchy:** a client/project strip and service overview lead to a detailed case study; the case study carries more trust than the homepage service list.
- **Service framing:** discovery, product repair, development, automation, data, leadership, and go-to-market are broad. The valuable common thread is clarity and ownership.
- **Founder visibility:** Abhinay and Divya are named with product and engineering backgrounds and a rationale for staying close to delivery.
- **Commercial path:** a free discovery call and email are supported by written scope, timing, cost, ownership, confidentiality, milestone, and scope-change terms.
- **Editorial pattern:** simple narrative sections; the case study uses problem, risk, intervention, before/after, timeline, testimonial, and fit.
- **Maz Works verdict:** emulate client ownership and the case-study sequence. Avoid offering nine service lines; one person cannot credibly foreground everything at once.

### 10. Swarm Labs — strongest products-plus-client-work integration

Official source: [Swarm Labs](https://swarmlabs.io/)

- **First five seconds:** it states UK custom software and automation, with applications, SaaS, APIs, and integrations tied to recovering human time.
- **Proof hierarchy:** live studio applications appear first; commissioned case studies then add implementation detail and output measures.
- **Service framing:** applications, APIs/integrations, plugins/extensions, and automation share a single operational outcome rather than separate brand stories.
- **Founder visibility:** weak. The registered company and studio are visible, but an accountable individual is not prominent. Maz Works should preserve the opposite.
- **Commercial path:** “tell us what you need built” connects to a short scoping route and work examples.
- **Editorial pattern:** short declarative headings, owned-product tiles, numbered capability rows, case-study outcome cards, an integration strip, and a single mission counter.
- **Maz Works verdict:** emulate the coherent mix of owned products and commissioned systems. Avoid the anonymous `we`, technology marquee, speculative mission counter, and metrics-as-decoration.

### Useful controls outside the direct peer set

- [Paravel](https://paravelinc.com/work/papajohns/) remains a strong case-study craft reference: initial condition, intervention, technical evidence, supporting images, and measured change. Its enterprise history is not comparable to Maz Works.
- [Good Enough](https://goodenough.us/posts/good-enough-a-serious-business) remains a strong identity reference: named authors, transparent decisions, and the feeling of a living small software workshop. It is not a client-services template.

## Cross-peer findings

### What the strongest peers do consistently

1. **They make the buyer’s problem recognizable before naming technology.** Side Quest focuses on costly workflows; Bluespace names spreadsheets and inboxes; Taylor lists specific tools a business might need.
2. **They label the relationship to each project.** Sidejar marks work as in-house or client; Maz Works should do the same consistently with `PRODUCT`, `CLIENT WORK`, and `LAB`.
3. **They show a person.** Taylor Make It, Dorio, and DejaDo make direct accountability part of the offer. Maz Works should clearly identify Manazir in the header/hero and include a real founder image or equally credible personal artifact when available.
4. **They reduce uncertainty with a bounded process.** DejaDo and KernelTheory explain fit, scope, ownership, launch, and what happens when a request falls outside the engagement. Maz Works already has a stronger, simpler version: Problem → Demo → Build → Proof.
5. **Their best evidence is visual and specific.** Adee Labs, Taylor Make It, and KernelTheory use shipped products, screenshots, direct links, and concrete before/after details. Lists of tools are supporting information, not the main proof.
6. **They offer one obvious next move.** Email, a short form, WhatsApp, or a call is prominent. The strongest sites do not make the visitor choose among six equally weighted CTAs.

### Patterns that look persuasive but should not be copied blindly

- Published starting prices can qualify leads, but they can also make the price—not the work—the dominant memory.
- “Founder-led” is only valuable when a name, face, role, and evidence support it.
- Response-time promises and availability banners are useful only if they can be honoured operationally.
- Testimonials are powerful only with permission and a traceable person/company.
- Quantified results should appear only where Maz Works can show how they were established.
- A process section helps, but repeating the same promise in the hero, service cards, FAQ, and final CTA adds noise rather than trust.

## Multi-perspective implications for Maz Works

### Employer perspective

Employers need to see judgment, implementation depth, and the ability to finish. Put architecture, trade-offs, tests, deployment, repository/live links, and honest limitations inside the flagship case studies. Keep the £150 client offer away from the first screen so it does not accidentally frame Manazir’s professional value as £150.

### Client perspective

Clients need to recognize their problem, trust the person doing the work, see commissioned proof, and understand the next step. Scrap Finance Partners should be the decisive evidence: real screenshots, what Manazir delivered, the working enquiry route, deployment, and the boundary that no commercial outcome is claimed.

### Collaborator perspective

Collaborators need to see taste, current curiosity, and openness. A compact Lab/current-work area and occasional build notes are more useful than another services grid. Good Enough’s active-workshop pattern is the best reference here.

### LinkedIn visitor perspective

A social visitor usually arrives with one claim in mind. Each strong project needs a stable deep link with a clear title, image, summary, live/code evidence, status, and limitation. The homepage should work as an index into those proofs rather than retell every full case study.

### Editorial/design perspective

The best transferable pattern is not a competitor’s palette. It is hierarchy: one dominant claim, one dominant project image, fewer but stronger cases, short labels, and spacious transitions. Maz Works’ warm ivory, near-black framework, thin dividers, and restrained yellow can remain distinctive while adopting that hierarchy.

### Conversion perspective

The primary journey should be `see work → trust the evidence → choose a route`. The free-demo offer should feel like a practical next step after proof, not an opening promotion. One compact final contact block can separate Hire / Client / Collaborate without three competing card funnels.

## Emulate, adapt cautiously, avoid

### Emulate

- Side Quest’s real-workflow language.
- Sidejar’s explicit `IN-HOUSE` versus `CLIENT` labels.
- Taylor Make It’s first-person founder accountability and concrete service examples.
- Adee Labs’ `work → proof → selective partner offer` sequence.
- KernelTheory’s problem/risk/intervention/before-after case-study sequence.
- DejaDo’s bounded fit, scope, and exclusion language.
- Bluespace’s “business before stack” discipline.
- Good Enough’s named authorship and active-workshop feeling.
- One direct contact action with a specific prompt: “What is slowing you down?”

### Adapt cautiously

- Leanline’s outcome buckets: reduce them to **Build**, **Automate**, and **Improve** only if they remain supported by visible projects.
- Published pricing: keep the settled £150 founding implementation, but place it inside the client section after proof and state the £75 + £75 boundary plainly.
- A founder portrait: use a real, current image only; otherwise use a signed/named text treatment until one exists.
- Availability or response expectations: publish only a commitment Manazir can consistently meet.
- Testimonials and client logos: add only with explicit permission.
- Build notes: begin with a small number of genuinely useful decisions, not a content-marketing backlog.

### Avoid

- Generic “AI-native agency” copy that could describe any competitor.
- Repeated “not an agency / no fluff / no theatre” posturing.
- Unverified revenue, savings, conversion, lead, user, or scale claims.
- Vanity metrics that do not prove delivered value.
- Technology walls, terminal mock-ups, and framework badges as primary proof.
- An SEO mega-page containing every service, location, industry, FAQ, and CTA.
- Fake scarcity, countdowns, “currently booking” banners, or guaranteed response times.
- Making the £150 offer the visual centre of the umbrella identity.
- Treating all projects as equal tiles.
- Copying any peer’s words, layouts, illustrations, imagery, or brand devices.

## Recommended Maz Works synthesis

The next implementation should use this public hierarchy:

1. **Header:** Maz Works, Manazir Hussain, Work, About, Client, Contact, GitHub.
2. **Hero:** one clear positioning sentence, one short supporting sentence, `View selected work` as the primary action, and a quieter `Show me the problem` link.
3. **Immediate proof:** at most three verified facts, not a large ledger.
4. **JobFilter flagship:** dominant image, problem, judgment/insight, build, proof links, status, limitation.
5. **Scrap Finance Partners:** equally credible but clearly marked `CLIENT WORK / SHIPPED`, with a real screenshot and enquiry/deployment evidence.
6. **Selected work:** Agent Nudge and OpenFlowKit as compact rows; smaller experiments remain in Lab or hidden.
7. **What Manazir builds:** three outcome-led lines at most—useful products, workflow automation, and focused AI tools.
8. **Process:** Problem → Demo → Build → Proof, with one sentence per step.
9. **Client offer:** bounded free demo, £150 founding implementation, £75 + £75 terms, and explicit out-of-scope protection.
10. **About:** real founder identity, working principles, current focus, and no inflated agency language.
11. **Contact:** one concise block with Hire / Client / Collaborate prompts feeding the same honest contact route.
